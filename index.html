Tips for Financial Cybersecurity

Protect your financial information with simple, effective steps.

Watch What You Click

Avoid clicking links or downloading attachments from unknown emails. Always go directly to the official website or app to verify messages.

Use Strong Passwords

Create unique, strong passwords for each account. Consider using a password manager to store them securely.

Enable Multifactor Authentication (MFA)

Add an extra layer of protection beyond just a password, like a code sent to your phone or biometric verification.

Maintain Device Security

Install antivirus, anti-spam, and anti-spyware software. Keep your system and apps updated to defend against new threats.

Use Your Own Device

Avoid accessing financial accounts from public or shared computers. If necessary, clear the cache and browsing history afterward.

Browse Carefully

Only log into accounts on secure HTTPS websites. Always log out fully after use.

Be Safe on Wi-Fi

Avoid public Wi-Fi for financial transactions. Secure your home network with strong encryption and a robust password.

Review All Correspondence

Regularly check statements and account activity. Report any unauthorized or suspicious activity immediately.

Use Security Software That Updates Automatically

Set antivirus and system software to update automatically to defend against new malware and vulnerabilities.

Treat Personal Information Like Cash

Only share sensitive details such as Social Security numbers or card information when absolutely necessary and only with trusted sites.

Check Company Legitimacy

Research unfamiliar companies or offers before providing information or engaging with them.

Give Personal Information Only on Encrypted Sites

Ensure the site uses HTTPS before submitting any sensitive information.

Protect Your Passwords

Use long, complex passwords with a mix of letters, numbers, and symbols. Avoid reusing passwords and never share them.

Back Up Your Files

Keep copies of important data on removable media or external drives so you can recover information if your system is compromised.
